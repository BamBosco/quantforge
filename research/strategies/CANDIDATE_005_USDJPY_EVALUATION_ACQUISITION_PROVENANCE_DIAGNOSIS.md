## Why the artifact looks like this

The header `<DATE>`, `<TIME>`, `<OPEN>`, `<HIGH>`, `<LOW>`, `<CLOSE>`,
`<TICKVOL>`, `<VOL>`, `<SPREAD>` is the MT5 Bars CSV layout. Its timestamps
show a mixed daily/hourly series despite the `M1` filename. Therefore the
filename reflects the selection shown in the MT5 workflow, not a verified
description of the exported rows.

`<SPREAD>` is present but is zero on every row. This means that this particular
MT5-exported series does not provide an observed per-bar spread usable for an
execution-price model. The available evidence cannot distinguish whether MT5
omitted/zero-filled spread for this historical series or whether the underlying
terminal data supplied zeros. Either way, the file is not adequate for that
purpose.

The CSV also has no side marker. It cannot establish whether its OHLC is BID or
ASK. These are provenance/data-representation failures, not a market-data
quality conclusion about Dukascopy as a provider.

## Correct Dukascopy acquisition path available

Dukascopy documents a historical-data facility for USD/JPY that permits:

- one-minute selection;
- explicit BID or ASK side selection;
- explicit GMT output instead of local time; and
- historical USD/JPY BID M1 access from `2003-05-04 21:00 UTC`.

This is the correct acquisition path for Candidate 005: use the Dukascopy
historical-data facility (web/JForex historical-data interface), not the MT5
Bars export workflow, and acquire separate native GMT/UTC M1 BID and ASK files
for the same frozen range. Pairing, schema, timestamp alignment, and
continuity must later be established through separately authorized
evaluation-only structural validation.

Sources: [Dukascopy historical-data manual](https://www.dukascopy.com/media/pdf/japan/manual/historical-data.pdf) and [Dukascopy IHistory API](https://www.dukascopy.com/client/javadoc/com/dukascopy/api/IHistory.html).
